Bedtime routine

How to add a sleep clock to a toddler bedtime routine.

A color clock works best as a predictable cue inside a routine rather than as the entire bedtime strategy.

Build a short repeatable sequence

A simple routine might be bath or wash-up, pajamas, tooth brushing, one or two books, then the sleep color. Repeating the same broad order can make bedtime more predictable.

Turn on the sleep cue before lights out

Let the child see the transition into the sleep color while you are still there. Remind them what the color means using the same short phrase each night.

Keep the display dim

Use enough brightness for the cue to be visible without making the device the brightest object in the room.

Use the wake color as part of morning

When the wake color arrives, reinforce it with normal morning activity. The transition becomes easier to understand when the household response matches the visual signal.

Expect practice

A sleep clock is a learned cue. Some children understand it quickly; others need repetition and age-appropriate support.

If sleep problems are persistent, severe, or associated with snoring, breathing problems, unusual daytime sleepiness or other health concerns, discuss them with the child's pediatric clinician.
